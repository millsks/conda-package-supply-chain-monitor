Stale is a property of a status, not a status

The chip keeps its hue, keeps its marker and keeps its word. It gains three things, and none of them is --warn on the chip's own hue.

What stale adds, and what it must never take away

  • A trailing amber hold mark. The ::before slot belongs permanently to the state's own marker, so the stale mark takes ::after — two 2px vertical amber bars, 2px apart, 8px tall, on the opposite side of the label. It reads as held: the conclusion stands, but it cannot be acted on until the evidence is re-collected.
  • An amber .ev footline naming the target it missed. The footline is real text where the mark is CSS-generated, so the meaning survives for a reader who cannot see the mark.
  • The word stale inside the chip, visually hidden. The visible label stays the bare OutcomeState value, so the closed set of status values stays separate from the open set of chip labels. Assistive technology announces "critical stale".
  • It takes nothing away. Not the hue, not the marker, not the word, not the legibility of the status. A strikethrough would degrade the status word, and the status word is a load-bearing channel.

The mark is rectilinear where the markers are not

All four sentinel markers are round or triangular; the hold mark is two straight bars. It sits on the right where they sit on the left, and it is --warn where they are currentColor. Three axes of difference, so it cannot be read as a sixth marker.

DESIGN §stale

The visible label never changes

Every chip in the stale column carries the same lowercase, verbatim OutcomeState value as its fresh neighbour. critical · stale is an illegal chip label; the qualifier lives on the footline.

A hidden stale rides inside the chip

<span class="vh"> stale</span> after the value, so the chip announces "critical stale" while reading critical. The ::after mark is CSS-generated and invisible to assistive technology, which is exactly why the word has to be real text.

a11y floor

Sentinels go stale too

Stale is computed per evidence row against that collector's freshness target, so an unknown whose evidence is past target is a stale unknown. It is not promoted, demoted or converted.

The ::before slot is not negotiable

It belongs permanently to the state's own marker. Any future overlay competing for it would have to displace a channel that carries the state itself.

CPM-FR-6

No strikethrough

The mockup's #states prose promised the conclusion would be struck through; line-through appears once in the whole file and not on a chip. It was also the wrong instrument — it degrades the status word.

A stale critical keeps --crit. A stale clean result keeps --ok.

This is the whole point of the correction. Under the old treatment both of these became the same amber chip, so a critical finding and a clean one looked identical the moment their evidence aged. Severity and currency are two axes, and staleness belongs to neither.

The hue did not move. A critical advisory that has not been re-checked is still a critical advisory. What changed is that it cannot be acted on: the transition table is evaluated against a stale finding, routing is not offered, and the primary control on the finding detail reads Recollect evidence.

orjson — a clean determinate result

none found fresh · --ok · filled dot
vulnerability_findings · observed 05:38Z
none found stale stale · --ok · filled dot · + hold mark
vulnerability_findings · observed 2026-08-29 · stale, target {{ collector.freshness_target }}

The hue did not move here either — and this is the harder half. A stale clean result is still the conclusion policy reached; turning it amber would say the finding got worse, and turning it grey would say we never asked. Both are false. It stays --ok, held, with the amber footline saying exactly what is out of date.

Why keeping --ok is not a softening

The reflex is that a stale clean result should be pushed toward a warning, because "we do not really know any more". That reflex is the CPM-SM-C1 failure mode running backwards: it treats a hue as the carrier of confidence, which puts staleness on the severity ramp and makes --warn mean two things again. The conclusion and its currency are separate facts and get separate channels — the hue carries the conclusion, the hold mark and the amber footline carry the currency, and the action the server offers carries the consequence.

Which is why the footline is real text

The ::after mark is CSS-generated and invisible to assistive technology, and desaturated in greyscale. The .ev.stale line spells out the staleness in words for both readers.

Two determinate rows on purpose

The first two rows are both determinate and both filled dots. They separate on the word alone, which is exactly why the word may never be moved into a title, an aria-label, an icon or a background image.

7

What --warn means now

One hue, one meaning. Everything that used to borrow it has somewhere else to be.

lagging

Determinate amber. A middle position on a severity or currency ramp. Also medium and manual review. A filled dot, because it is a conclusion.

P3

The p3–p4 priority band. Bold mono, no marker, no border — visually distinct from a chip at a glance, which is what stops a priority being read as a status. What the buckets contain is undefined in every source.

inventory-derived

Package-identity confidence. Text only, no chip, no marker. A determinate middle position, shown normally and labelled. The label annotates the row; it never degrades the value.

critical stale

The stale hold mark. --warn appears on the chip, but as a 6×8px overlay in the trailing slot — never as the chip's hue, its border or its marker.

blocked

Readiness, not outcome. ready / blocked / unknown, markerless, in its own column. A fix that exists nowhere yet is blocked — distinct from ready and from unknown.

collectors 7 of 8 healthy

Not a chip at all. A string that belongs to no enumeration is not a chip — it is a freshness-bar cell or body text. It may still take --warn as a text colour, because there it is prose about currency, not a status on a value.

Five outcome states, and there will never be a sixth

blocked, stale, superseded, skipped and every workflow state are not among them. Each is a property, an axis or an enumeration of its own. The stale overlay exists precisely so that expressing "this conclusion is held" never requires inventing a state to hold it in.

The theme switch at the top of this page sets data-theme on the root element and is a specimen affordance for reviewing both palettes side by side. v1 ships no theme toggle — the application honours prefers-color-scheme only, and nothing in it sets data-theme. The hook is kept in the CSS for a later preference surface (G-5). It is the only script in this file.
